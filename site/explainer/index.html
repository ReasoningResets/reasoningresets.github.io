An animated intuition for Theorem 1

Where should a
reset land?

Begin with conventional outcome-based RL: one trajectory, one final reward, and no way to revisit an earlier decision. Then introduce resets—and see why choosing the right reset state produces two distinct theoretical gains.

↓ Build the argument

01 · Conventional outcome-based RL

One trajectory produces one outcome.

Contemporary RL with verifiable rewards assigns a single outcome reward across the tokens in a reasoning trajectory. That signal says whether the final answer succeeded, but it does not reveal which intermediate decision was responsible.

The policy moves forward from prompt to answer. It never returns to an earlier state to ask what would have happened differently.

02 · Introduce the reset

Hold the past fixed. Vary the future.

A reset re-enters a previously visited state and resamples a continuation. Multiple continuations now share the same history and differ only after the reset state.

Differences in their outcomes can be attributed to the decisions made from that state. A reset turns one observed future into a counterfactual comparison.

03 · Now choose where to reset

A policy passes through many states.

Conservative Policy Iteration draws states from the policy’s on-policy visitation distribution. Once resets are available, a random reset can land on any state the current policy reaches.

Every moment is a possible intervention point. The theorem asks whether they are equally useful.

04 · Find where gain lives

Only some states have room to improve.

A state is τ-improvable when the policy admits an action whose advantage is at least τ. Let pπ be the on-policy probability of reaching this improvable set 𝒢.

If one in ten visited states is improvable, then pπ = 0.1. The useful signal is real—but rare.

05 · See the dilution

Random resets average signal with silence.

Random-reset sampling draws from every visited state. An advantage of at least τ appears only with probability pπ, so the minimum guaranteed advantage is diluted to τpπ.

At pπ = 0.1, nine of ten resets may spend expensive rollouts where the guaranteed gain does not live.

06 · Condition on what matters

The oracle preserves the full signal.

A credit-assignment oracle is a membership test for improvable states. Conditioning resets on 𝒢 changes the estimation target from τpπ back to τ.

The oracle does not create a better action. It stops averaging that action’s signal with states that have no guaranteed signal.

07 · Square the estimation gain

Why does sample complexity improve by 1/pπ2?

Statistical error shrinks like 1/√n. Random resets must resolve a target as small as τpπ; targeted resets resolve τ. Making the target 1/pπ larger therefore needs 1/pπ2 fewer samples.

To see a signal ten times smaller at the same confidence, you need one hundred times as many samples.

08 · Improve where the signal lives

Targeting also yields more progress per iteration.

Credit-aware CPI applies its conservative update on the improvable set rather than everywhere. In the regime highlighted by the theorem, this increases the guaranteed per-iteration improvement by 1/pπ.

One factor comes from estimating a concentrated signal; the other comes from applying the update where that signal lives.
