Credit assignment with targeted resets

We analyze resets within Conservative Policy Iteration and compare random-reset sampling with a credit-assignment oracle that targets improvable states.

01 / Setup

The oracle identifies states that admit a better action.

A state is τ-improvable when the policy admits an action with advantage at least τ. The quantity pπ is the on-policy probability of reaching the improvable set 𝒢.

01 Improvable states

𝒢 contains states with maximum action advantage at least τ.

When pπ is small, random-reset CPI spends most of its sampling budget on states without a guaranteed advantage of τ.

Example: pπ = 0.1

02 Advantage estimation

Conditioning on 𝒢 removes the pπ dilution factor.

Random-reset CPI has a global guaranteed advantage of τpπ. The credit sampler conditions on the improvable set and has conditional guaranteed advantage τ.

03 Policy update

Credit-oracle CPI applies its conservative update on the improvable set.

The larger conditional advantage reduces the number of Q-rollouts required for estimation. Restricting the update to 𝒢 also permits a larger conservative step.

02 / Approximate credit sampler

A practical localizer may select states outside the improvable set.

Exact oracle access is an idealization. An approximate credit sampler conditions on a selected set 𝒮, which need not equal the true improvable set 𝒢.

The two localization errors enter different parts of the bound.

False discoveries reduce the conditional advantage. Missed improvable states reduce the on-policy mass on which the update is applied.

03 / Practical instantiation

SRPO uses explicit self-localization in place of the oracle.

In the RLVR setting, the model identifies the first erroneous thought in a failed trajectory and resets to the verified-correct prefix preceding that error.
